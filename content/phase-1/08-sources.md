# Editorial sources and verification notes

Checked October 23, 2026. These notes support content drafting; they do not verify the client’s licence, appointment, competence, actual services, or operating policies. Research summaries are intentionally brief. The service-page process descriptions are original proposed practice copy, not summaries of official sources.

## S1 — Small Claims Court limit

[Ontario regulation: Small Claims Court jurisdiction and appeal limit](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/000626)

The maximum claim amount is $50,000 under the consolidation effective October 1, 2025. Use this for the limit only; an amount within the limit does not establish that every dispute belongs in that court.

## S2 — Provincial offence response options

[Ontario: Check traffic tickets, fines, and response options](https://www.ontario.ca/page/check-status-traffic-tickets-and-fines-online-or-request-meeting-resolve-your-case)

Supports the statement that voluntary payment of an offence notice is a guilty plea. Available choices depend on the document and process. The draft avoids a universal response deadline or universal insurance prediction.

## S3 — Accident-benefits coverage changes

[FSRA: After an Accident — Understanding the Claims Process](https://www.fsrao.ca/consumers/auto-insurance/protect-yourself/after-accident-understanding-claims-process)

FSRA describes accident benefits and changes for policies entered into on or after July 1, 2026. The draft directs readers to the actual policy and rules, rather than treating every benefit as universally included.

## S4 — Accident-benefits tribunal

[Tribunals Ontario: LAT-AABS application and hearing process](https://tribunalsontario.ca/lat-aabs/application-and-hearing-process/)

Supports identifying LAT-AABS as a forum for disputes under the Statutory Accident Benefits Schedule. Specific eligibility and deadlines are not determined in the copy.

## S5 — Refugee Appeal Division representation

[IRB: Do I need to be represented at the RAD?](https://irb.gc.ca/en/filing-refugee-appeal/Pages/representation.aspx)

Explains eligible paid counsel, including qualifying provincial law-society members, and emphasizes that filing responsibilities and time limits still apply.

## S6 — IRB counsel and refugee appeals

[IRB: My Case user guide](https://mycase-mondossier.irb-cisr.canada.ca/en-US/faq/)

Identifies Ontario paralegals among possible paid counsel subject to good standing.

[IRB: Filing a refugee appeal](https://www.irb-cisr.gc.ca/en/filing-refugee-appeal/filing-a-refugee-appeal/pages/index.aspx)

Supports distinguishing a RAD appeal from seeking Federal Court review, for which the IRB points readers toward a lawyer. No individualized appeal-eligibility conclusion is made.

## S7 — LSO verification still required

[LSO: Paralegal licensing FAQs](https://lso.ca/becoming-licensed/paralegal-licensing-process/faqs)

[LSO: By-Law 4](https://lso.ca/about-lso/legislation-rules/by-laws/by-law-4)

Both direct pages returned 403 during this drafting session. They are listed as follow-up references, not as successfully inspected evidence. The refugee service list remains expressly subject to client scope confirmation. Do not broaden the page into general immigration applications on the strength of the IRB sources alone.

## S8 — LTB notice and eviction distinctions

[LTB: How a Landlord Can End a Tenancy](https://tribunalsontario.ca/documents/ltb/Brochures/How%20a%20Landlord%20Can%20End%20a%20Tenancy.html)

Supports distinguishing a notice from an eviction order and the requirement for the appropriate enforcement process.

## S9 — N4 notice

[LTB: N4 notice instructions](https://tribunalsontario.ca/documents/ltb/Notices%20of%20Termination%20%26%20Instructions/N4_Instructions.html)

Supports describing the N4 as a notice concerning non-payment of rent. No universal notice period or individual validity assessment is given.

## S10 — HRTO jurisdiction

[HRTO: Application and hearing process](https://tribunalsontario.ca/hrto/application-and-hearing-process/)

Supports describing the HRTO as dealing with discrimination and harassment claims under Ontario’s Human Rights Code.

## S11 — Protected grounds and areas

[HRTO: Applicant’s Guide](https://tribunalsontario.ca/documents/hrto/Guides/HRTO-Applicants_Guide.html)

Explains the need to connect an application to covered grounds and social areas. The draft does not label all workplace unfairness as a Code violation.

## S12 — Notarization and commissioning

[Ontario: Information for notaries public](https://www.ontario.ca/page/information-notaries-public-ontario)

Explains the distinction between a notary and commissioner and the physical-presence requirement for notarization. Specific document acceptance and the client’s appointment still require confirmation.
