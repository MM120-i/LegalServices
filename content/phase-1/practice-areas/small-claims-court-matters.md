## Is Small Claims Court the right place?

Ontario Small Claims Court currently hears eligible claims for money or the return of personal property valued at $50,000 or less. The monetary limit increased on October 1, 2025. Being within the limit does not, by itself, establish that the court is the correct forum for every dispute.

If your concern involves another tribunal, a different kind of remedy, or a larger claim, discuss the appropriate process before filing. Do not give up part of a claim merely to fit a limit without understanding the consequences.

## Prepare the facts and documents

Write down who the dispute involves, what was agreed, what happened, and what result you are seeking. Collect supporting records in date order. Include documents that do not support your position as well as those that do; an assessment needs the full picture.

If you were served with a claim, keep the complete package and note when and how you received it. Seek advice about the response requirements promptly. Sending Lexora an inquiry does not file a defence or stop the court process.

## Proposed approach

1. Identify the dispute, parties, amount, and relevant dates.
2. Review the evidence and discuss the available process and practical costs.
3. Agree on the proposed work and fees if the practice accepts the matter.
4. Prepare for the agreed steps, which may include pleadings, settlement, trial, or enforcement.

## Questions

### Can you help if I am being sued?

Ask about defence services and provide the response date immediately. Acceptance depends on the matter, availability, and any conflict of interest.

### Does winning mean I will receive the money immediately?

No. A judgment and collecting payment are separate matters. Enforcement may require further steps, costs, and an assessment of what is practical.

### Will my dispute go to trial?

Not every dispute does. Settlement may be discussed, but neither settlement nor a particular court outcome can be promised.
