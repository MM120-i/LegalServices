# Traffic Tickets & Provincial Offences

Route: `/practice-areas/traffic-tickets-provincial-offences`

H1: Traffic ticket and provincial offences paralegal in Scarborough.

You have received a ticket or summons, and you need to decide what to do next. Before paying or choosing a response, understand the charge, the instructions on your document, and the possible consequences.

Bring the ticket or summons and note any response date before calling the Scarborough office. Those details help identify the charge, the court process and the help you need before choosing a response.

## What you can ask about

- Speeding, red-light, and handheld-device charges.
- Careless driving and other Highway Traffic Act charges.
- Driving without insurance charges.
- Provincial offences and municipal by-law matters.
- Reviewing the ticket, summons, and available disclosure.
- Preparing for resolution discussions or a trial, where appropriate.

## Before choosing your response

For an Ontario provincial offence notice, paying the amount on the ticket is a guilty plea. Other response options depend on the document and the court process. A summons or an administrative penalty may require a different approach.

A fine is only one concern. Ask about possible licence and driving-record consequences, and discuss insurance questions with your insurer or broker. Demerit points and insurance pricing are different issues; a particular result on one does not guarantee a result on the other.

Read the response instructions promptly. Do not assume that asking someone for advice extends the time available to respond.

## Prepare for your conversation

Have a clear copy of the ticket or summons, the date you received it, any listed court date, and any correspondence available. If there are several charges, include all the documents. A short account of what happened can help identify what needs closer review.

Do not send identity documents or detailed personal records through the initial website form. Ask how to provide them.

## Proposed approach

1. Identify the charge, the document type, and the relevant dates.
2. Discuss the available process and the proposed work and fees.
3. If retained, review the evidence and prepare the agreed response or representation.
4. Explain the result and any remaining obligations within the agreed retainer.

## Questions

### Can you guarantee the charge will be withdrawn?

No. A result depends on the evidence, the applicable law, and the decisions made in the proceeding. No particular outcome is promised.

### Will a ticket affect my insurance?

That depends on the circumstances and your insurer’s rules. Do not assume that a charge without demerit points has no insurance consequences.

### I already paid. Can I still call?

Yes. Tell the office what you paid and when. Whether any further step is available requires an individual assessment; it should not be assumed.
